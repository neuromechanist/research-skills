# NSF Review Criteria - Complete Reference

## Panel Review Process

### How NSF Review Works
1. Program officer selects reviewers (ad hoc or panel)
2. Each proposal reviewed by 3-5 panelists
3. Panelists write individual narrative reviews
4. Panel discusses proposals and assigns ratings
5. Panel provides a summary rating
6. Program officer makes final funding decision considering:
   - Panel ratings and narrative
   - Portfolio balance
   - Strategic priorities
   - Available budget

### Reviewer Roles
- **Lead reviewer**: Presents the proposal to the panel, summarizes key points
- **Other panelists**: Read, score, and discuss all assigned proposals
- **Program officer**: Moderates discussion, does not score but makes final decision

## Two Core Merit Review Criteria

### 1. Intellectual Merit
**Key questions:**
- What is the potential for the proposed activity to advance knowledge and understanding within its own field or across different fields?
- To what extent do the proposed activities suggest and explore creative, original, or potentially transformative concepts?
- Is the plan for carrying out the proposed activities well-reasoned, well-organized, and based on a sound rationale?
- How well qualified is the individual, team, or institution to conduct the proposed activities?
- Are there adequate resources available to the PI?

**What reviewers assess:**
- Scientific rigor and feasibility of the approach
- Creativity and novelty of the research questions
- Track record and expertise of investigators
- Appropriateness of the methodology
- Quality of preliminary results (if any)
- Likelihood of achieving the stated objectives

**Common strengths:**
- Novel research questions with clear significance
- Well-designed methodology with appropriate controls
- Strong track record of the PI in the proposed area
- Access to unique resources or populations

**Common weaknesses:**
- Incremental research with limited potential for impact
- Methodology not well suited to the research questions
- Insufficient justification for the proposed approach
- Missing or weak preliminary data for ambitious claims

### 2. Broader Impacts
**Key questions:**
- What is the potential for the proposed activity to benefit society or advance desired societal outcomes?
- To what extent do the proposed activities suggest and explore creative, original, or potentially transformative concepts?
- Is the plan for carrying out the proposed activities well-reasoned, well-organized, and based on a sound rationale?
- How well qualified is the individual, team, or institution to conduct the proposed activities?
- Are there adequate resources available to the PI?

**What reviewers assess:**
- Clear plan for societal impact beyond the scientific community
- Integration of research and education
- Broadening participation of underrepresented groups
- Enhancement of infrastructure for research and education
- Dissemination plan for research results
- Benefits to society at large

**Common strengths:**
- Concrete, measurable broader impacts activities
- Integration of research into educational programs
- Meaningful plans for broadening participation
- Partnerships with organizations serving underrepresented groups

**Common weaknesses:**
- Broader impacts as an afterthought (generic mentoring statement)
- No concrete plan or timeline for BI activities
- BI activities disconnected from the research
- No assessment plan for BI effectiveness

## Rating System

### Individual Reviewer Ratings
NSF uses descriptive ratings, not numeric scores:

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Excellent** | Outstanding proposal in all respects; deserves highest priority for support |
| **Very Good** | High-quality proposal in nearly all respects; should be supported if possible |
| **Good** | Quality proposal with some weaknesses; worthy of support |
| **Fair** | Some merit but has one or more significant weaknesses |
| **Poor** | Proposal has serious deficiencies; should not be supported |

### Panel Summary Rating
The panel reaches consensus on an overall rating:
- **High Competitive** / **Competitive** / **Low Competitive** / **Not Competitive**
- Or: **Fund** / **Consider** / **Do Not Fund**
(Terminology varies by directorate)

### Key Differences from NIH
- **Narrative-based**: No numeric scores; emphasis on written justification
- **Two criteria only**: Intellectual Merit + Broader Impacts (vs. NIH's five)
- **Program officer discretion**: PO has more latitude than at NIH
- **Portfolio balance**: Funding decisions consider the overall portfolio
- **No percentile system**: Rankings are relative within the panel
- **Broader Impacts weighted equally**: Not a secondary consideration

## Review Narrative Structure

### What a Good NSF Review Contains
1. **Summary**: Brief description of the proposed work
2. **Intellectual Merit assessment**: Specific strengths and weaknesses
3. **Broader Impacts assessment**: Specific strengths and weaknesses
4. **Summary rating**: Overall assessment with justification

### What Reviewers Are Told to Avoid
- Personal attacks or unprofessional language
- Requesting specific changes (this is a fund/not-fund decision, not revision)
- Reviewing the budget (that's the PO's job)
- Comparing to other proposals in the panel

## Proposal-Specific Criteria

### Standard Research Grants
- Primary criteria: IM + BI
- Results from Prior NSF Support evaluated within IM

### CAREER Awards
- Additional emphasis on integration of research and education
- Career development plan evaluated
- Educational activities must be integral, not add-on

### RAPID/EAGER
- Higher risk tolerance
- Focus on urgency (RAPID) or high-risk/high-reward (EAGER)
