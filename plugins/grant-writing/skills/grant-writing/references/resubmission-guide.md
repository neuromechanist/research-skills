# Resubmission Guide

## Overview

A resubmission (A1) is a revised application submitted after an unsuccessful initial (A0) submission. NIH allows one resubmission per application. The resubmission must include an Introduction page that summarizes changes made in response to reviewer critiques.

---

## Introduction Page

### Format
- **1 page maximum** (strictly enforced)
- Same formatting as the rest of the application (Arial 11pt, 0.5in margins)
- Placed before the Specific Aims page in the assembled application
- Titled "Introduction to Revised Application" or "Introduction"

### Structure

**Opening (1-2 sentences):**
Briefly state the application number, original submission date, and overall summary of the revision.

**Point-by-Point Responses:**
Organize responses by reviewer. Address every substantive critique, even those disagreed with. Use a structured format:

```
**Reviewer 1:**

*Critique: [Paraphrase the reviewer's concern concisely]*
**Response:** [Describe the change made or provide a rebuttal with rationale. Reference the specific section and page where the change appears.]

*Critique: [Next concern]*
**Response:** [Response]

**Reviewer 2:**
[Same format]

**Reviewer 3:**
[Same format]
```

### Writing the Introduction

- Acknowledge the review panel's effort and the value of the feedback (one sentence, not effusive)
- Prioritize: address the most significant critiques first within each reviewer's section
- Be concise; the 1-page limit demands efficiency
- Reference where changes appear: "See Approach, page 8" or "See revised Aim 2, page 10"
- If a critique cannot be fully addressed (e.g., requiring years of additional data), explain what was done and provide a reasonable plan

---

## Point-by-Point Response Format

### For Critiques Addressed by Changes
- Paraphrase the critique (do not copy verbatim from the summary statement)
- State the specific change made
- Reference the location in the revised application
- Example: *"Reviewer 2 noted insufficient power analysis. We have added a formal power analysis for each aim based on effect sizes from our preliminary data (Approach, p. 9-10)."*

### For Critiques Addressed by Rebuttal
- Acknowledge the concern
- Provide evidence or reasoning for the current approach
- Add clarifying text to the application if possible, even when disagreeing
- Example: *"Reviewer 3 questioned the feasibility of the proposed timeline. While we respectfully maintain that the timeline is achievable given our team's demonstrated throughput (see Preliminary Data, p. 5), we have added milestones and go/no-go decision points to clarify progress benchmarks (Approach, p. 12)."*

### For Minor or Incorrect Critiques
- Do not dismiss or argue combatively
- Briefly clarify the misunderstanding
- Add text to the application to prevent the same misreading
- Example: *"We appreciate this concern. The sample size referenced by Reviewer 1 (N=20) was the pilot cohort; the proposed study enrolls N=120. We have clarified this distinction in the Approach (p. 7)."*

## Change Marking Conventions

### NIH Expectations
- Changes must be clearly identifiable in the revised application
- Two acceptable approaches:

**Option 1: Bracketed text**
Mark changed passages with brackets or a note: `[Revised text addressing Reviewer 2, Critique 3]`

**Option 2: Vertical change bars**
Place vertical lines in the margin next to revised text. In LaTeX, use the `changebar` package or manual `\marginpar` annotations.

### Best Practices
- Mark substantive changes only (not minor rewording or typo fixes)
- Do not mark the entire document; excessive marking defeats the purpose
- Ensure marks are visible but do not distract from readability
- Remove all change marks before the final A0 submission of any future application

## Strategy for Addressing Critiques

### General Principles
- Treat every critique as an opportunity to strengthen the application
- Never respond defensively or dismissively, even to critiques perceived as unfair
- Make real changes; reviewers can tell the difference between substantive revision and cosmetic edits
- The same reviewers may or may not review the resubmission; write for any expert reader

### When Reviewers Disagree with Each Other
- Acknowledge both perspectives
- Explain the approach taken and why
- Cite literature or preliminary data to support the chosen direction

### When a Critique Requires Major Restructuring
- Consider whether the reviewer identified a genuine weakness
- Restructure if doing so strengthens the science
- If the restructuring changes the scope, ensure the revised aims are still internally consistent
- Update the Specific Aims page, budget, and timeline to reflect changes

### When Preliminary Data Are Requested
- Generate the requested data if feasible within the resubmission timeline
- If not feasible, provide related data or a well-powered pilot study
- Explain a clear plan for generating the data in the early phase of the project

### When Feasibility Is Questioned
- Add preliminary results demonstrating capability
- Strengthen the timeline with milestones and contingency plans
- Add letters of support from collaborators or core facilities
- Include go/no-go decision points

---

## Common Mistakes in Resubmissions

- Exceeding the 1-page Introduction limit (application may be returned without review)
- Ignoring a critique (reviewers notice and penalize omissions)
- Being combative or dismissive toward reviewer feedback
- Making only superficial changes ("We have clarified the text" without substantive revision)
- Failing to update the budget and timeline to match revised aims
- Not marking changes in the body of the application
- Submitting the resubmission to a different study section without explanation
- Forgetting that the A1 must stand on its own; new reviewers may not have seen the A0

---

## Timeline and Logistics

- A1 must be submitted within 37 months of the A0 receipt date
- Plan to submit the A1 at least one cycle after receiving the summary statement
- Allow 2-4 months for revision after receiving reviews
- If the A1 is not funded, the next submission must be a new A0 (with a new project number)
- Contact the program officer before resubmitting to discuss the revision strategy
