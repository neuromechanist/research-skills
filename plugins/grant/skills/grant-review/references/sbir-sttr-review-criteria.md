# NIH SBIR/STTR Review Criteria

Review criteria for Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR, activity codes R43 and R44) and Small Business Technology Transfer (STTR, activity codes R41 and R42) applications.
These differ from research project grants in framework, in criteria content, and in what a weakness costs.

> **Scope note.** The three-factor **Simplified Review Framework** (NOT-OD-24-010) in `nih-review-criteria.md` covers Research Project Grants only.
> Its activity-code list does **not** include R41, R42, R43, or R44.
> Small-business applications continue to be scored on the **five classic criteria**, each 1 to 9, plus an overall impact score.
> Do not apply the three-factor framework when reviewing a small-business application.

## Key differences from an R01 review

- **Commercial potential is scored, not noted.** It appears inside Significance, Investigator(s), Innovation, and Environment, and again as an additional criterion through the Commercialization Plan.
- **Milestones replace hypotheses.** Reviewers are asked whether the application states clear, appropriate, measurable goals to be achieved before the next phase. An exploratory aims page is a weakness here, not a style choice.
- **The panel is a special emphasis panel grouped by technology**, with names of the form "Small Business: Non-HIV Diagnostics, Food Safety, Sterilization/Disinfection, and Bioremediation" or "Small Business Applications: Drug Discovery and Development". Reviewers span the technology area, so domain-specific writing loses readers who still score.
- **Additional criteria depend on the application type**: Phase I milestones; Phase I progress for a Phase II; Fast-Track acceptability; the Commercialization Plan for anything other than a standalone Phase I.
- **Preliminary data are not required for a standalone Phase I**, but are expected in the Phase I portion of a Fast-Track.
- **Budget deviations are a review matter.** Statutory budget and duration guidelines can be exceeded with justification; the committee comments when they are exceeded without it.

## Scored criteria, with the small-business questions

Each criterion is scored 1 (exceptional) to 9 (poor). The scale and its descriptors match `nih-review-criteria.md`.

### 1. Significance

Standard question: does the project address an important problem or critical barrier?

Small-business additions:

- Phase I: does the project have commercial potential to lead to a marketable product or service?
- Phase II, Direct to Phase II, and Fast-Track: does the Commercialization Plan demonstrate a high probability of commercialization?
- Is the unmet need described from the customer's position, with the current alternative named and its cost stated?

Reviewers routinely fold market conditions into this criterion, positively and negatively.
In the funded sample set, one reviewer's Significance strengths cited the size of the addressable problem and the failure of the incumbent product;
another reviewer's Significance weakness on a different application was that the compound could not yet be manufactured in pure form at scale, a commercial risk raised inside a scientific criterion.

### 2. Investigator(s)

Standard question: are the principal investigators and team suited to the project?

Small-business additions:

- Does the team hold commercialization expertise, not only scientific expertise?
- For later-stage projects, is there someone whose job is business development, partnering, or regulatory strategy?
- For STTR, is the division of labor between the small business and the research institution sensible, and does the leadership plan match it?

The sharpest example in this sample set is an overall-impact paragraph on a scientifically excellent STTR Phase I that listed three rectifiable weaknesses, in order:
no business leadership in the relevant therapeutic space, no business development plan to turn the award into a viable small business, and no letters of interest from industry stakeholders.
That reviewer had recorded no weaknesses under any of the five scored criteria.

### 3. Innovation

Standard question: does the project challenge current practice or use novel concepts, approaches, or methods?

Small-business additions:

- Is the innovation defensible? For Phase II and Fast-Track, reviewers are asked whether the small business presents a reasonable plan to create a temporal barrier against competitors.
- Is the advantage over incumbent products specific and technical, rather than a claim of general superiority?

Note that a reviewer can find the underlying idea unoriginal and still score Innovation well if the specific implementation is new, and the reverse.
In the sample set, reviewers separated the target from the means: blocking a given interaction was judged unoriginal, while the specific method proposed for blocking it was judged novel, and the score followed the method.

### 4. Approach

Standard question: are the strategy, methodology, and analyses well-reasoned and appropriate?

Small-business additions:

- Phase I and the Phase I portion of a Fast-Track: are there clear, appropriate, measurable goals, that is, milestones, to be achieved before initiating Phase II?
- Phase II: how well does the application demonstrate progress toward meeting the Phase I objectives?
- Are go/no-go decision points defined, with the action taken if a threshold is missed?
- Is the product development path, including screening cascade, target product profile, and regulatory route, laid out where the product requires one?

Where reviewers praised Approach in this sample set, they named artifacts:
a screening cascade, a target product profile, explicit go/no-go decisions, a serious rather than perfunctory treatment of potential problems and alternatives.
Where they criticized it, the pattern was almost always a missing number: undefined success metrics, no named baseline for comparison, unstated sample sizes, unstated yields.

### 5. Environment

Standard question: will the scientific environment contribute to the probability of success?

Small-business additions:

- Phase I: does the small business have appropriate business expertise and resources, or has it identified where it will get them?
- Phase II and Fast-Track: does the applicant have access to the business experts and resources needed to commercialize?
- Are the collaborating institution, contract research organizations, and specialized facilities documented, including access to any containment or manufacturing capability the plan requires?

Environment weaknesses in this sample set were rarely about laboratory space.
They were about capability the company would need later:
a reviewer noting that additional preclinical and clinical development personnel would be required before a Phase II submission,
or that facilities descriptions at the partner institution were too thin to judge.

## Additional review criteria

Assessed and commented on, generally not scored 1 to 9 on their own, but they shape the overall impact score and appear in the summary statement.

| Criterion | Applies to | What reviewers ask |
|---|---|---|
| Commercialization Plan | Phase II, Direct to Phase II, Phase IIB, Fast-Track, Commercialization Readiness Pilot | Is the market opportunity presented with segments? Are post-award milestones and the plan to reach them credible? Is there a funding plan, and were letters of interest or commitment obtained? Is the management team suited to commercialization? Is the route to adoption and revenue plausible? |
| Phase I milestones | Phase I, Fast-Track | Are the goals clear, appropriate, and measurable, and would they be achieved before Phase II begins? |
| Phase I progress | Phase II, Phase IIB | Did the Phase I meet its objectives, and is the Phase II a logical extension of the result? Summary statements carry an explicit "Phase II (Type 2 R42 and Type 2 R44 applications)" block with an acceptable or unacceptable finding. |
| Fast-Track acceptability | Fast-Track | Is combining the phases justified, and is the transition between them governed by measurable criteria? A reviewer can rate the science highly and still find Fast-Track unacceptable. |
| Human subjects, vertebrate animals, biohazards, select agents | As applicable | Standard NIH treatment, plus containment access where the pathogens require it |
| Authentication of Key Biological and Chemical Resources | As applicable | Present and adequate. Marked unacceptable strikingly often in this sample set, including on applications with otherwise no noted weaknesses |
| Resource Sharing plans | As applicable | Present and adequate |
| Budget and period of support | All | Recommended as requested, modified, or flagged. Statutory guideline overruns require justification in the application; the committee comments when justification is absent, and accepts an explicitly elected waiver |

## Recurring criticism patterns

Compiled from the six NIAID small-business summary statements listed in the `grant-writing` skill's `references/niaid-sample-applications.md`.
Use these as a checklist when reviewing; they recur across phase, activity code, and decade.

| Pattern | Where it lands | What the reviewer actually says |
|---|---|---|
| No commercialization team | Investigator(s), Environment | Business leadership in the relevant space is absent; the company will need staff it does not have before the next phase |
| No business development plan | Overall impact, Environment | Nothing explains how this award becomes a viable business |
| No industry or customer letters | Overall impact, Commercialization Plan | Letters of interest from stakeholders are absent, or their timing is unexplained |
| Success metrics undefined | Approach | Metrics for the key comparison are not described in detail; no baseline method is named, which weakens rigor |
| Aim ordering forces rework | Approach | A later sub-aim changes the material used by an earlier one, so the earlier experiments may have to be repeated |
| Feasibility risk unmitigated | Approach | The plan's fallback would not rescue the failure mode it is offered for |
| Manufacturing or scale-up ignored | Significance, Approach | Purity, yield, and separation problems could hamper commercial development |
| Administrative attachment missing | Additional criteria | Authentication plan not provided; resource sharing plan not provided |
| Budget over cap without justification | Budget | The amount is reasonable, but the cap overrun was not justified as required |
| Phase I not finished | Phase I progress | The Phase I is under a no-cost extension; it is unclear whether Phase II is contingent on completing the Phase I objectives |
| Deployment claim outruns the technology | Significance, Approach | The chemistry is too complex for the point-of-care setting claimed; the product will stay in well-equipped laboratories |
| Sex as a biological variable unaddressed | Vertebrate animals, Approach | Only one sex will be used; the choice may be defensible, but it was not discussed |

## Reviewing well: calibration notes

- **Do not penalize a Phase I for lacking preliminary data.** Do penalize it for lacking milestones.
- **Do not reward a beautifully written science plan that has no business plan.** The mechanism exists to commercialize; an application that treats commercialization as paperwork is missing the point of the award, and that belongs in the critique.
- **Separate "the market is hard" from "this application does not understand the market."** The first is context; the second is a weakness.
- **Check the numbers on the aims page.** If you cannot tell from the milestone whether the aim succeeded, that is a specific, fixable weakness worth writing down.
- **Read the Commercialization Plan against the Research Strategy.** Technical milestones should map onto product requirements. When they do not, one of the two documents was written by someone who did not read the other.
- **Score the science on the science.** Reviewers in the sample set gave 1s across scientific criteria while writing hard business critiques in the overall impact paragraph. That is the correct shape.

## Output notes

Use the standard NIH output template in `review-output-templates.md`, with these adjustments:

- Five criterion scores, not three factor scores.
- Add an Additional Review Criteria block covering, as applicable: Commercialization Plan, Phase I milestones, Phase I progress, Fast-Track acceptability, and the administrative attachments.
- State the application type explicitly at the top: Phase I, Phase II, Direct to Phase II, Phase IIB, or Fast-Track, with the activity code. The criteria applied depend on it.
