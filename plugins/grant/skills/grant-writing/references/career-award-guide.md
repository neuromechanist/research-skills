# NSF CAREER Award Guide

## Overview

The NSF Faculty Early Career Development (CAREER) Program supports early-career faculty who exemplify the role of teacher-scholar through integration of research and education. CAREER is the most prestigious NSF award for junior faculty.

---

## Eligibility

- Hold a tenure-track (or equivalent) position at a US institution
- Hold a doctoral degree
- Have not previously received a CAREER award
- Check the current solicitation for exact eligibility windows (typically untenured, within first few years of appointment)

---

## Proposal Structure

### Project Summary (1 page)
- Overview, Intellectual Merit, and Broader Impacts paragraphs
- Must clearly convey both the research and education components

### Project Description (15 pages)
- Results from Prior NSF Support (if applicable, within the 15 pages)
- Integrated Research and Education Plan (the core of the proposal)
- Assessment and Evaluation Plan

### Additional Required Documents
- **Departmental letter:** A letter from the department chair (or equivalent) confirming institutional commitment, mentoring support, and resources
- References Cited
- Biographical Sketch and Synergistic Activities
- Budget and Budget Justification
- Facilities, Equipment, and Other Resources
- Data Management Plan
- Mentoring Plan (if graduate students or postdocs are supported)

---

## Research-Education Integration

### The Central Requirement
The research and education plans must be **bidirectionally integrated**, not merely co-located in the same proposal. The research must inform the education, and the education must advance or contribute to the research.

### What Bidirectional Integration Means
- Research outcomes feed into course materials, outreach programs, or training curricula
- Educational activities generate data, insights, or trained personnel that advance the research
- The two components share themes, methods, populations, or datasets
- Removing either component would weaken the other

### Examples of Strong Integration
- Developing computational tools for research that also serve as teaching platforms for undergraduates
- Engaging students in data collection or analysis that directly contributes to research aims
- Creating community partnerships for both outreach and research data gathering
- Training workshops where participants produce results used in the research

### Common Weaknesses
- Education plan that is a standard outreach activity bolted onto a research proposal
- Research and education sharing a topic but having no functional interaction
- Education limited to mentoring graduate students already on the project (this is expected, not distinctive)
- Vague assessment plan for educational outcomes

---

## Educational Plan Standards

### Required Elements
- Clear educational objectives tied to the research
- Target audience (undergraduate, K-12, community, graduate)
- Specific activities with timeline
- Assessment plan: how to measure whether educational goals are met
- Sustainability: how the educational component continues beyond the award

### Assessment and Evaluation
- Use established assessment instruments when available
- Define measurable outcomes (not just "students will learn about X")
- Consider pre/post assessments, longitudinal tracking, or comparison groups
- External evaluation is recommended for the educational component

### Broader Impacts Through Education
- Broadening participation of underrepresented groups in STEM
- Improving STEM education at any level
- Developing new educational materials or curricula
- Public engagement with science

## Budget and Scope

### Duration and Funding
- 5-year award (fixed duration)
- Budget: varies by directorate, typically $400K-$800K total
- Check the specific solicitation and program officer guidance for typical award sizes in the relevant directorate
- Budget should reflect genuine 5-year scope; reviewers flag budgets that appear inflated or thin

### Budget Considerations
- Support for graduate students and/or postdocs conducting the research
- Costs for educational activities (materials, assessment tools, participant support)
- Conference travel for both research dissemination and education conferences
- Equipment and supplies aligned with the research plan
- Assessment/evaluation costs for the education component

---

## Departmental Letter

### Purpose
Demonstrate institutional commitment to the PI's career development and to the proposed project.

### Should Address
- Commitment to mentoring the PI (name the mentor if possible)
- Teaching release or protected research time during the award
- Access to facilities, space, and equipment
- How the institution values and supports integration of research and education
- Tenure and promotion considerations related to the CAREER activities

### Common Mistakes
- Generic letter that could apply to any PI
- Letter that does not mention the specific proposed activities
- Missing commitment to tangible support (reduced teaching, lab space, etc.)

---

## Review Criteria

### Standard NSF Criteria Apply
- **Intellectual Merit:** significance and novelty of the research, PI qualifications, adequacy of resources
- **Broader Impacts:** primarily evaluated through the educational plan and its integration with research

### CAREER-Specific Considerations
Reviewers are specifically instructed to evaluate:
- Quality and significance of the research plan
- Quality and feasibility of the educational plan
- **Degree of integration** between research and education (this is the distinguishing criterion)
- PI's potential for leadership in research and education
- Institutional commitment (evidenced by the departmental letter)

---

## Writing Tips

- Frame the proposal as a cohesive vision, not as two separate plans stapled together
- Use a single narrative thread that weaves research and education throughout
- Begin the Project Description with a compelling vision statement that encompasses both components
- Address integration explicitly; do not rely on reviewers to infer connections
- Include a figure or diagram showing how research and education components interact
- Be specific about educational activities (who, what, when, how many, how assessed)
- Demonstrate awareness of education research and assessment literature relevant to the proposed activities
- Position the CAREER award as a foundation for a long-term career trajectory in both research and education
