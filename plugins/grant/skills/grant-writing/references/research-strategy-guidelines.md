# NIH Research Strategy Guidelines

## Overview

The Research Strategy is the heart of an NIH application. It directly corresponds to the three primary review criteria:

| Section | Review Question | Typical Length (R01) |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **Significance** | Why is this important? | 1.5-2 pages |
| **Innovation** | What is new/novel? | 1-1.5 pages |
| **Approach** | How will you do it? Can you do it? | 7-9 pages |

**Total Page Limit:** 12 pages for R01, 6 pages for R21

**Key Insight:** Approach has the highest correlation with overall impact score.

---

## Significance Section

### Purpose
Convince reviewers this is an important problem worth solving. Answer "So what?" and "Who cares?"

### Structure
1. **Importance of the problem** - Scope, burden, scientific importance
2. **Current state of knowledge** - What we know, key literature
3. **Gaps and limitations** - What we don't know, why others haven't solved it
4. **Critical barrier** - THE key obstacle the research addresses
5. **How this project addresses the gap** - Connect aims to gaps
6. **Potential impact** - Scientific, clinical, broader impacts

### Tips
- The farther reviewers are from the field, the more background is needed
- Put significance in context of: (1) field state, (2) long-term plans, (3) preliminary data
- Be explicit about what changes if the project succeeds

---

## Innovation Section

### Purpose
Explain why the concepts, methods, or approach are novel to the field.

### Three Types of Innovation
1. **Conceptual** - New theoretical framework, paradigm shift
2. **Technical** - New methods, tools, technologies
3. **Methodological** - New study design, analytical approach

### Structure
- State each innovation explicitly (can use bullet points)
- Explain WHY each is innovative (not just WHAT)
- Connect innovations to solving the identified gaps

### Tips
- Avoid overselling; reviewers are experts
- If methods aren't novel, innovation can come from application or combination
- For high-innovation projects, consider R21, DP1, DP2 mechanisms

## Approach Section

### Purpose
Show WHAT will be done, HOW it will be done, and that the team CAN do it.

### Structure

#### 1. Preliminary Studies (~1.5-2 pages)
- **Critical for R01** - establishes feasibility
- Include figures with preliminary data
- Show the team has the skills, tools, and access needed
- Directly support that proposed experiments can work

#### 2. Aims (organized by Specific Aims)
For each aim:
- **Rationale** - Why this aim? Scientific justification
- **Methods** - Detailed experimental design
  - Specific protocols
  - Sample sizes with power analysis
  - Controls and comparisons
  - Data analysis plan
- **Expected Outcomes** - What to anticipate finding
- **Interpretation** - What results mean (both directions)
- **Potential Problems & Alternatives** - Contingency plans

#### 3. Rigor & Reproducibility
- Scientific rigor (blinding, randomization, controls)
- Consideration of biological variables (sex, age)
- Authentication of key resources
- Data management and sharing

#### 4. Timeline & Milestones
- Gantt chart or milestone table
- Realistic timeline with dependencies
- Clear go/no-go decision points

### Tips
- Reviewers scrutinize approach most heavily
- Be specific about methods; vagueness suggests insufficient thought
- Address potential problems before reviewers raise them
- Show how aims are interconnected but not dependent

## Writing Best Practices

### Organization
- Use bold headers consistently throughout
- Start each section with the header (Significance, Innovation, Approach)
- Use numbering/outline system for complex methods
- White space improves readability

### Visual Elements
- Figures are essential; reviewers scan them first
- Use figures for: conceptual overview, preliminary data, methods workflow
- Tables for: comparisons, timelines, personnel
- Keep figures clean and readable at reduced size

### Figures Strategy
- **Figure 1: Project Overview** - Place at start of Approach, show conceptual framework, ~1/3 page
- **Preliminary Data Figures** - One figure per major preliminary result, publication quality
- **Methods Figures** - Workflow diagrams, experimental design schematics, timeline/Gantt charts

## Common Pitfalls

### Significance
- Too narrow (only relevant to one lab)
- Too broad (solving world hunger)
- Not connecting to NIH mission or IC priorities

### Innovation
- Claiming novelty without evidence
- Confusing "no one has done this" with "this is innovative"
- Not explaining WHY innovation matters

### Approach
- Insufficient preliminary data
- Methods too vague or too detailed
- Not addressing obvious potential problems
- Overly ambitious timeline
- Aims too interdependent (domino effect if one fails)

## Review Criteria Mapping (RPG Simplified Review Framework, NOT-OD-24-010)

For Research Project Grants with due dates on or after 2025-01-25, reviewers evaluate three factors. Write each section knowing which factor it feeds:

| Factor (scoring) | Rolls in | Where Addressed |
|------------------|----------|-----------------|
| Factor 1 - Importance of the Research (scored 1-9) | Significance + Innovation | Significance section + Innovation section + throughout |
| Factor 2 - Rigor and Feasibility (scored 1-9) | Approach | Approach section |
| Factor 3 - Expertise and Resources (assessed, not scored) | Investigators + Environment | Biosketch + Preliminary Studies + Facilities |

Fellowship (F) and career development (K) awards use their own revised frameworks, not these three factors; see the `grant-review` skill's `nih-career-training-criteria.md`.
