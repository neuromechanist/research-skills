# The SBIR/STTR Commercialization Plan

A business plan, submitted inside a grant application, and scored.

**Maximum 12 pages** (verified against the NIH Table of Page Limits on 2026-08-07; confirm against the live table and your Notice of Funding Opportunity, or NOFO, before filing).
**Required for** Phase II, Direct to Phase II, Phase IIB, Phase I/Phase II Fast-Track, and Commercialization Readiness Pilot applications.
**Not allowed** with a standalone Phase I application.

You need not use all 12 pages. The funded plans in the NIAID sample set run well short of the limit.

## Where it lands in review

The Commercialization Plan is evaluated as an additional review criterion, and its substance also feeds the scored criteria.
On the current parent SBIR NOFO, the Significance criterion asks Phase II and Fast-Track reviewers directly whether the Commercialization Plan demonstrates a high probability of commercialization,
the Investigator criterion asks whether the team holds commercialization expertise,
and the Environment criterion asks whether the company has access to the business experts and resources needed to commercialize.
A weak plan therefore does not merely lose an unscored bullet; it pulls down criteria that carry scores, on an application whose science may be excellent.

Practical consequence: the plan is not a formality to draft last.
If you cannot yet write a convincing one, that is an argument for a Phase I with an I-Corps at NIH commitment rather than a Fast-Track or a Direct to Phase II.
See `sbir-sttr-requirements.md`.

## The required section outline

NIH prescribes the headings. Use them verbatim, in this order, so a reviewer can find each part.

1. **Value of the SBIR/STTR Project, Expected Outcomes, and Impact**
2. **Company**
3. **Market, Customer, and Competition**
4. **Intellectual Property (IP) Protection**
5. **Finance Plan**
6. **Production and Marketing Plan**, and **Revenue Stream**

Funded plans in the sample set follow this outline closely.
The Lochhead Phase II plan (MBio Diagnostics, R44, Forms-B2) uses the lettered form of these headings almost exactly;
the Harty Phase II plan (Fox Chase Chemical Diversity Center, R42, Forms-F) uses the numbered form and splits Production and Marketing from Revenue Stream into separate sections.
Older plans, such as Liu (TherapyX, R44, Forms-D), organize around the same content under their own headings, opening with a disease and burden overview before market analysis.
Prefer the prescribed headings; the outline is what reviewers are told to look for.

## Section by section

### 1. Value of the project, expected outcomes, and impact

What the technology does, what it will be as a product, and who is better off.

Funded plans put three things here:

- **Key technology objectives** stated as product specifications rather than research goals.
- **A target product profile.** The strongest version in the sample set is a two-column table, preferred and minimally acceptable, with a row per attribute: efficacy, safety, route or format, dosing or throughput, stability, resistance or failure mode. It makes the milestone thresholds in the Research Strategy legible as product requirements rather than arbitrary numbers.
- **A dated path to market**, step by step, each step carrying its expected quarter, its cost, and its size. In the Harty plan each regulatory and clinical step names the quarter, the participant count, and the dollar cost, which is what makes the timeline checkable rather than aspirational.

Close with impact: who is treated, tested, or served that is not served today, and what the award does for the company and its staffing.

### 2. Company

Corporate objectives, core competencies, present size, products with meaningful sales, prior federal and non-federal funding, regulatory experience,
and the path from a research and development shop to a commercial entity.

Funded plans subdivide this section, and the subdivisions are worth copying:

- **Leadership**, naming who owns commercialization, not only who owns the science. Both strong Phase II plans in the sample set name a business development lead with industry launch experience alongside the scientific team. A reviewer flagged the absence of exactly this on an otherwise excellent STTR Phase I.
- **Funding history**, prior awards and their outcomes.
- **Business strategy**, how the company makes money from this product line.
- **Regulatory strategy**, the specific pathway and its evidence requirements.
- **Reimbursement strategy**, where a payer stands between the product and the customer. Diagnostics and therapeutics plans that skip this look naive.

SBIR applicants with prior Phase II awards must additionally attach a **Company Commercialization History**, which is a separate document from this plan.
It lists, for each prior Phase II: awarding agency, award number and date, award amount, project title, the source, date, and amount of any non-SBIR/STTR follow-on funding, and the commercialization status.

### 3. Market, customer, and competition

The market segments targeted, the plan to win customer acceptance, and an analysis of competition.

What separates funded plans from weak ones here:

- **Segmentation with sizing per segment**, not one blended total addressable market number. Funded plans size the beachhead segment separately and cite the source for each figure.
- **Named customers**, with what they buy today and what it costs them. Letters of support from those customers belong in the Letters of Support attachment, and the plan should point at them.
- **A competitor table**: one row per named competing product, with a column stating your specific advantage over it. Generic claims of superiority score worse than a table admitting where a competitor is stronger.
- **Barriers to entry** you hold, and how long they last.

### 4. Intellectual property protection

Patent and provisional patent status, licenses in and out, and freedom to operate.

Funded plans present issued and pending filings as a small table: title, number, jurisdiction, status or issue date.
They also state what new filings the proposed work will generate and when they will be prepared,
and they say plainly which claims cover the specific compound, device, or method being developed versus the earlier platform.

**STTR only:** the small business and the research institution must have an agreement allocating intellectual property rights between them.
Funded STTR plans reference this agreement by name in the plan itself.
A reviewer looking for it and not finding it will say so.

### 5. Finance plan

How development and commercialization get paid for after the award ends, and what evidence exists that someone else will pay.

Funded plans include:

- **Current funding status**, prior awards with periods.
- **Named alternative commercialization models**, not one assumed exit. The Harty plan lays out three: license the asset to a larger company; carry the program further internally and license later at a higher valuation; a hybrid where a partner takes over mid-programme. Each names who pays, roughly when, and what evidence unlocks that route.
- **Non-dilutive follow-on sources** relevant to the field, named specifically rather than gestured at.
- **Letters of commitment or intent** where they exist, referenced here and attached.
- **Specific steps being taken to secure Phase III funding.** Phase III means commercialization funded from any source other than SBIR/STTR; NIH does not fund Phase III.

Reviewers ask directly whether the application presents a funding plan and whether letters of interest or commitment were obtained.
If none exist yet, say what you are doing to get them and by when. Silence reads worse than an honest gap.

### 6. Production and marketing plan, and revenue stream

Manufacturing, marketing, licensing, and sales channels; then how revenue is actually generated.

Funded plans state current internal production capability with numbers,
name the point at which an external contract manufacturer takes over and what certification that requires,
describe how awareness is built in the customer community, and enumerate revenue routes:
direct sales, distributors, joint ventures, licensing with milestones and royalties, government procurement, and the staffing each route requires.

## What reviewers say about weak plans

Drawn from the summary statements in the NIAID small-business sample set, plus the review criteria in the current parent NOFO.

| Criticism | What triggers it | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No business leadership on the team | Every named person is a scientist | Add a named commercialization lead with launch or business development experience, in the biosketches and in Section 2 |
| No plan to turn the award into a viable business | Science plan with a market paragraph bolted on | Write Sections 5 and 6 as a real financing and revenue plan, with dates |
| Absence of letters of interest from industry or customers | Letters are all academic collaborators | Collect customer and partner letters early; they take longer than any internal document |
| Commercial route unclear or single-threaded | One assumed acquirer, no alternative | Name two or three routes with the evidence that unlocks each |
| Manufacturing or scale-up threatens the product | Purity, yield, or cost of goods unaddressed | Address production explicitly, including cost of goods, in Section 6 |
| Company will need staff it does not have | Plan implies capabilities beyond the current team | State the hiring plan and its trigger points |
| Market claims unsourced | Numbers with no citation | Cite every market figure; primary evidence beats analyst summaries |

Two further observations from the sample set worth internalizing.
First, commercial risk does not stay inside the Commercialization Plan;
reviewers raised manufacturing purity and staffing gaps inside the Significance, Approach, and Environment critiques of applications whose science they praised.
Second, the compliments are as specific as the complaints:
where a plan was praised, reviewers named the concrete artifacts, the screening cascade, the target product profile, the go/no-go decisions, the alternative commercialization models.
Those artifacts are what a good plan is made of.

## Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] All six prescribed section headings present, in order, using NIH's wording
- [ ] Within the page limit in the NIH Table of Page Limits and the NOFO
- [ ] Target product profile table with preferred and minimally acceptable columns
- [ ] Dated path to market with cost and scale per step
- [ ] Market sized per segment, every figure cited
- [ ] Competitor table with named products and per-competitor advantages
- [ ] Intellectual property table with status per filing; STTR allocation-of-rights agreement referenced
- [ ] Named commercialization lead with relevant business experience
- [ ] At least two funding routes after the award, with triggers
- [ ] Customer or partner letters of interest referenced here and attached in Letters of Support
- [ ] Company Commercialization History attached, if any prior SBIR Phase II award exists
- [ ] Every technical milestone in the Research Strategy traceable to a product requirement here
