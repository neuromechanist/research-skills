# Budget Justification Guide

## Overview

The budget justification provides narrative explanation for every line item in the budget. Reviewers and program officers use it to assess whether costs are reasonable, necessary, and directly tied to the proposed work. A weak justification can sink an otherwise strong proposal.

## Modular vs Detailed Budgets

### Modular Budget (NIH)
- Use when requesting up to $250K direct costs per year
- Specify in $25K modules (e.g., 3 modules = $75K)
- Provide brief personnel justification only (role, effort, no salaries)
- No detailed categorical breakdown required
- Most R01 and R21 applications use modular format

### Detailed Budget (NIH)
- Required when requesting >$250K direct costs per year
- Itemize every category with specific dollar amounts
- Required for: most R24, multi-PI grants over threshold, cooperative agreements
- NSF always requires detailed budgets

## Personnel

### Effort and Salary
- Report effort in person-months (calendar, academic, or summer)
- Calendar months = total annual effort (1 month = 8.3% effort)
- Academic months = 9-month appointment effort
- Summer months = effort during summer period only
- Justify the level of effort for each person relative to their role

### NIH Salary Cap
- NIH limits salary reimbursement to the Executive Level II rate
- As of 2026, verify the current cap at the NIH Office of Extramural Research
- If an individual's institutional salary exceeds the cap, budget only at the cap rate
- The institution covers the difference

### Fringe Benefits
- Apply the institutional fringe benefit rate to each personnel category
- Common categories: faculty, staff, postdocs, graduate students (each may have different rates)
- Include health insurance, retirement, FICA, workers' compensation as applicable
- State the rate and its basis (e.g., "Fringe benefits for faculty are calculated at 28.5% per institutional rates")

### Typical Personnel Justification Format
```
[Name/TBD], [Title], [X% effort / Y person-months]
[Brief description of role and responsibilities on the project.
Justify why this level of effort is needed.]
Salary: $XX,XXX | Fringe (XX.X%): $X,XXX | Total: $XX,XXX
```

## Equipment vs Supplies

### Equipment (>$5,000 per unit)
- Items with a unit cost of $5,000 or more and a useful life of more than one year
- List each piece of equipment separately with cost and justification
- Explain why existing equipment cannot be used
- Equipment is typically excluded from indirect costs
- Example: "One [instrument name] ($XX,XXX) is required for [specific experimental need]. No suitable instrument is available at the institution."

### Supplies (<$5,000 per unit)
- Consumable materials, items under $5,000
- Group by category: laboratory supplies, computing supplies, animal costs, participant compensation
- Provide cost basis (e.g., "$X per assay x N assays = $Y")
- For large supply budgets, break down by aim or major activity

## Travel

### Domestic Travel
- Justify each trip: purpose, destination (if known), number of travelers
- Conference travel: name the conference, explain relevance to the project
- Typical budget: airfare + hotel + per diem x number of days
- Follow institutional or federal per diem rates

### Foreign Travel
- Requires additional justification: explain why the travel is essential
- State the country and purpose
- NIH: foreign travel must be specifically identified in the budget
- Some funding mechanisms restrict or prohibit foreign travel

---

## Consultant Costs

- Identify consultants by name or role
- State daily or hourly rate and estimated time commitment
- Justify why the expertise is not available within the project team
- Include travel costs for consultants separately if applicable
- Provide a letter of support from each named consultant
- Format: "[Name], [Affiliation], [Expertise]. Rate: $X/day x Y days = $Z. [Justification for this expertise.]"

---

## Subawards / Consortium Costs

- List each subaward site separately
- Provide a full budget and justification from each subrecipient
- First $25,000 of each subaward is subject to indirect costs at the prime institution
- Costs above $25,000 per subaward are excluded from the prime's indirect cost base
- Include scope of work, PI effort, and institutional resources for each site
- Each subrecipient must provide a letter of intent and an institutional approval letter

---

## Other Direct Costs

### Common Categories
- **Publication costs:** page charges, open-access fees
- **Participant support costs (NSF):** stipends, travel, subsistence, registration fees for participants (excluded from indirect costs at NSF)
- **Human subject costs:** IRB fees, participant compensation, screening costs
- **Animal costs:** per diem, veterinary, surgical supplies
- **Computing:** cloud computing, software licenses, data storage
- **Tuition remission:** for graduate research assistants (if institutional policy requires)

---

## Indirect Costs (Facilities & Administrative)

- Apply the institution's federally negotiated indirect cost rate
- The rate and base (e.g., modified total direct costs) come from the institution's rate agreement
- Common exclusions from the indirect cost base: equipment, tuition, subaward costs above $25K, participant support costs
- State the rate, the base, and the resulting indirect cost amount
- Example: "Indirect costs are calculated at XX.X% of modified total direct costs per the institutional rate agreement dated [date]."

---

## Common Mistakes

- Listing costs without narrative justification
- Requesting equipment available at the institution
- Inconsistency between budget numbers and the justification text
- Forgetting to include fringe benefits or applying the wrong rate
- Not accounting for annual salary increases (typically 3% per year)
- Omitting indirect costs on subaward first-$25K amounts
- Budgeting participant support costs with indirect costs applied (NSF excludes these)

---

## NIH vs NSF Key Differences

| Category | NIH | NSF |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Budget format | Modular (<$250K/yr) or Detailed | Always Detailed |
| Salary cap | Executive Level II cap applies | No federal salary cap (2/9 rule for senior personnel) |
| Participant support | Included in other direct costs | Separate category, excluded from indirect |
| Equipment threshold | $5,000 | $5,000 (verify institutional policy) |
| Tuition | Varies by institution | Often included as direct cost |
| Cost sharing | Generally not required | Generally not required; do not offer voluntary cost sharing |
