# Responding to Reviewer Comments

## Response Letter Structure

```
Dear Editor,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise our manuscript titled "{TITLE}"
(Manuscript ID: {ID}). We appreciate the constructive feedback from the
reviewers. Below we provide point-by-point responses to each comment.
Changes in the revised manuscript are highlighted in {blue/tracked changes}.

## Reviewer 1

### Comment 1.1
> {Quote the reviewer's comment verbatim}

**Response:** {Your response}

We have revised {section/page/line} to address this concern. The revised
text now reads: "{quote revised text}"

### Comment 1.2
> {Quote the reviewer's comment}

**Response:** {Your response}

## Reviewer 2

### Comment 2.1
...

Sincerely,
{Authors}
```

## Response Strategies

### Agree and Revise
"We thank the reviewer for this insightful suggestion. We have revised the manuscript accordingly. Specifically, we {describe the change} (page X, lines Y-Z)."

### Partially Agree
"We appreciate this comment and partially agree. While {acknowledge valid point}, we note that {reason for partial disagreement}. We have addressed this by {compromise action}."

### Respectfully Disagree
"We thank the reviewer for raising this point. However, we respectfully maintain our original approach because {reason 1} and {reason 2}. {Cite supporting evidence}. We have added text to clarify our rationale (page X, lines Y-Z)."

### Request for Additional Experiments
"We have conducted the suggested analysis. The results {confirm/extend/modify} our original findings (new Figure S{N}/Table S{N}). We have incorporated these results into the revised manuscript."

## Common Reviewer Concerns and Templates

### "Sample size is too small"
"We acknowledge this limitation and have added a power analysis to the Methods section (page X). Our sample of N={X} provides {Y}% power to detect effects of size d={Z} at alpha=0.05. We have also added this as a limitation in the Discussion."

### "Statistical analysis is inappropriate"
"We have revised our statistical approach. We now use {new method} instead of {old method} because {reason}. The results {remain consistent / have changed as follows}."

### "Missing relevant citations"
"We thank the reviewer for pointing us to this literature. We have incorporated the suggested references and added discussion of {findings from those papers} in the Introduction/Discussion (page X)."

### "Methods lack detail for reproducibility"
"We have expanded the Methods section to include {specific details}. We have also added {supplementary protocol/code repository link} to ensure reproducibility."

## Tone Guidelines

- Always thank the reviewer, even for critical comments
- Never be defensive or dismissive
- Quote comments verbatim so the editor can track responses
- Indicate exact locations of changes (page, line, section)
- If a change affects multiple locations, list all of them
- Be concise but thorough
